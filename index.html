Book a Reformer AssessmentQuestions about your back

Before you get on the reformer, we read your health history.

Who it works with

  • Pain.Back pain that comes and goes, or that never quite leaves.
  • Recovery.Coming back from an injury or surgery, often after physical therapy.
  • Caution.Anyone who has been told to build strength carefully.

We are a Pilates studio, not a clinic. If you're unsure whether a class is right for you, check with your physical therapist, surgeon or doctor first.

How a class adapts

  1. 1

    Your form

    You tell us about your back on the health form. Arrive ten minutes early for your first visit to fill it in.

  2. 2

    Your level

    We place you at your level and choose the springs and positions that suit you.

  3. 3

    Your corrections

    In class your teacher watches how you move and corrects it as you go.

For clinicians

Physical therapists, surgeons and doctors: refer a patient and we'll be in touch to arrange their first visit.

Questions about your back

Is Pilates safe if I have back pain?

Many people with back pain take Reformer for a Healthy Back. Tell us about your back on the health form, and if you're unsure, check with your doctor or physical therapist first. We read every form before your first class.

Do I need a referral?

No. If you have one from a physical therapist or surgeon, bring it with you.

Can I come after surgery?

Once your surgeon or physical therapist has cleared you for exercise. Put the surgery and its date on your health form.

What should I wear?

Fitted exercise clothes. Grip socks are encouraged, and the studio sells them. Please avoid strong fragrance. Equipment is provided; bring water.

Where should I start?

With a Reformer Assessment: forty-five minutes, one to one, with a written plan for where to begin.

Brand guidelines

ROOT PILATES COMPANY, formerly the Pilates Barre. How to use the name, the marks, the colours and the type, for Sharon, the team, and anyone designing for the studio.

This page isn't in the menu. Share the link with the people who need it.

The name

In full
ROOT PILATES COMPANY
The old name
Pair the name with "formerly the Pilates Barre" for the first twelve months, so long-time clients and search engines can find the studio. confirm the date it drops
In sentences
Always in capitals, in headings and in running text alike: ROOT PILATES COMPANY. In a long paragraph, give it a touch of extra letter-spacing.
Don't
Shorten it to initials, or add "The" in front of it.

The marks

The stacked lockup at the top of this page is the primary mark.

Clear space
At least the height of the O in ROOT on every side.
Smallest size
Stacked lockup 120px wide on screen (30mm in print). Horizontal 160px (40mm). Emblem 32px (8mm).
Motion
The emblem may turn, always as one piece, and it always comes back upright. The five figures are hand-placed, not evenly spaced, so the parts never move separately for long.
Don't
Stretch, recolour outside the palette, add shadows or outlines, rearrange the parts, or set a mark over a busy photo without a solid ground.

In use

How the marks carry onto signage and studio goods. Mockups, for direction: neither shows the studio as it stands.

Signage
The horizontal lockup at reception, in full colour on a cream wall, with the emblem raised as its own disc.
Tote
The full stacked lockup, formerly line included, on natural canvas.
Patches
The emblem on its cream disc, on Forest and on Unmuted Violet. The disc keeps the figures readable on any colour.
Bottle and labels
The wordmark on its own. On a dark label, the type reverses to Cream.
Grip socks
ROOT alone works on small goods, where the full name is close by, on the packaging or at the desk.
